01 / The question

Can a flexible data center
change the value of a grid upgrade?

Data centers can shift electricity demand across the day. Transmission upgrades can relieve network constraints. This study asks how their modeled benefits change when voltage, reactive power, and losses enter the optimization.

Four matched counterfactuals isolate flexibility, a C6 line-rating increase, and their joint effect. Each is evaluated under linear DC and nonlinear AC network models on the synthetic RTS-GMLC benchmark.

03 / Joint value

Do the interventions reinforce
each other—or overlap?

The answer can depend on the network model. On 3 of 27 selected days, the sign of the flexibility–upgrade interaction reverses between DC and AC. Those reversals persist at both power factors.

I = CF + CR − CFR − C0

I > 0 · Complementarity
The joint benefit exceeds the sum of separate benefits.

I < 0 · Substitution
The interventions overlap in the value they provide.

These are operating-cost interactions, not infrastructure investment returns.

The AC interaction is less than 1.6% of combined savings on each reversal day. A change in sign matters for interpretation; its economic magnitude also matters.

05 / Methodology

A controlled comparison,
with explicit assumptions.

01

Prepare inputs

Chronological demand and renewable availability from RTS-GMLC. Select 27 days spanning demand and congestion conditions.

02

Match cases

Identical daily energy, intervention definitions, and generation inputs. Evaluate all four cases at PF 1.00 and 0.95.

03

Solve & compare

HiGHS for DC; multistart IPOPT for AC. Minimize operating cost, then maximize VRE within a tight cost cap.

Validation details and practical limits

The archive records all 1,080 primary AC starts and 216 secondary solves as optimal and feasible. Maximum recorded independent nodal residuals are below 10−6 MW/MVAr; recorded thermal, voltage, and generation bound violations are zero. Primary cost spread across starts is less than $0.000018.

These checks support numerical consistency; they do not certify global AC optimality. The archive contains saved diagnostics rather than complete raw AC states for an independent reconstruction of every network equation.

No unit commitment, ramping, storage energy dynamics, N−1 security, interday demand shifting, or capital costs are included. Generator minimum active power is zero; reactive bounds are independent of active output. The rating change is a modeled capacity intervention, not a complete physical line reconstruction.

06 / What this establishes

Model simplicity is a choice.
Its consequences are measurable.

The contribution is a reproducible way to quantify when network-model fidelity changes the apparent value of demand flexibility and transmission capacity.

The selected-day results support DC as a useful screening model under these assumptions. AC adds a check on losses, voltage, reactive constraints, and day-specific intervention rankings. Neither comparison establishes a universally best model or real-world investment profitability.

RTS-GMLC is a synthetic research benchmark. These findings describe this test system and these selected days. Broader claims require additional systems, operating constraints, and validation.
